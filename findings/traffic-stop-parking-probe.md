# Probe: identifying yourself at a traffic stop, and paying a parking ticket

Two questions that sound similar and are not. One is the strongest identity duty an ordinary
law-abiding person encounters in Utah; the other has no state identity duty at all.

**Caveat specific to this probe.** The traffic-stop question is the one topic in this repo where
**case law does much of the work**, and case law is a fourth body of law held in none of the corpora.
The statutes below set the outer frame; what a court will actually enforce depends on Fourth and
Fifth Amendment doctrine (*Hiibel v. Sixth Judicial District Court*, 542 U.S. 177 (2004)) and on Utah
appellate decisions construing §76-8-301.5. Read what follows as the statutory text, not as the law
in operation.

## Traffic stop — yes, and it is unusually specific

Three provisions interlock.

**1. The officer's power to demand.** §77-7-15:

> A peace officer may stop any individual in a public place when the officer has a **reasonable
> suspicion** to believe the individual has committed or is in the act of committing or is attempting
> to commit a public offense and **may demand the individual's name, address, date of birth, and an
> explanation of the individual's actions.**

Note what this is: a grant of authority to *ask*. It states no duty to answer and carries no penalty.

**2. The duty, and it is narrower than the demand.** §76-8-301.5, "Failure to disclose identity":

> (2) An actor commits failure to disclose identity if, during the period of time that the actor is
> **lawfully subjected to a stop as described in Section 77-7-15**:
> (a) a peace officer demands that the actor disclose the actor's **name or date of birth**;
> (b) the demand … is **reasonably related to the circumstances justifying the stop**;
> (c) the disclosure … **does not present a reasonable danger of self-incrimination**; and
> (d) the actor fails to disclose the actor's name or date of birth.
> (3) A violation of Subsection (2) is a **class B misdemeanor**.

The asymmetry between the two sections is the finding. §77-7-15 lets an officer demand **name,
address, date of birth, and an explanation of your actions**. §76-8-301.5 criminalises refusing only
**name or date of birth**. Declining to give an address, or to explain yourself, is not an offence
under this section — the officer may ask, but the compulsion does not extend that far. Three further
conditions narrow it: the stop must be lawful, the demand must relate to the reason for the stop, and
the self-incrimination proviso at (2)(c) carves out exactly what the Fifth Amendment would.

Critically, the duty is to **disclose** — to *say* who you are. Nothing in §76-8-301.5 requires
producing a document or proving the assertion. It is compelled **identity assertion**, backed by
criminal penalty. The same design as the fishing licence, with a sharper enforcement edge.

**3. Drivers, separately, must produce a document.** §53-3-217:

> (1)(a) The licensee shall have his license certificate in his **immediate possession at all times
> when driving** a motor vehicle.
> (1)(b) A licensee shall **display his license certificate upon demand** of a justice of peace, a
> peace officer, or a field deputy or inspector of the division.
> (3) A person who violates Subsection (1)(a) or (1)(b) is guilty of an **infraction**.

This is a genuine document-presentation duty, and the reason a traffic stop feels different from
every other interaction surveyed in this repo. But it attaches to the **licensee driving a vehicle**,
not to people generally. A passenger, or someone stopped on foot, owes name or date of birth under
§76-8-301.5 and no document at all.

Note also §53-3-217(2): producing a valid licence in court is a **defence** to the charge. The
offence is about the licence's availability at the roadside, not about whether the state knows who
you are.

## Paying a parking ticket — no identity requirement in state law

**Nothing found.** Searching the statutes and all 661 court rules for fine-payment language occurring
near identity language returns **zero** hits.

The payment mechanism is designed to avoid the person entirely. §77-7-21(1)(b) permits an individual
to "**voluntarily pay the recommended fine amount and other penalties without a personal appearance
before the court**" for any class B misdemeanour or lower — which covers parking infractions. You pay
against a citation number. No identity element appears anywhere in the transaction.

Two structural reasons this differs from the traffic stop:

**Parking is delegated to local government.** §41-6a-208 gives local highway authorities power over
"*regulating or prohibiting stopping, standing, or parking*," and §41-6a-214 extends local ordinance
authority to quasi-public parking areas. So the operative parking rule and its payment mechanics are
**municipal ordinance** — outside all three corpora, and outside state law entirely. What a given city
requires to pay online is its own policy, exactly as with utility connections.

**The citation attaches to a vehicle, not to an identified person.** §77-7-20(2)(b) requires a
citation to contain "*the name and date of birth of the individual cited*" — which presupposes
someone present to be cited. A parking ticket is issued to an empty car. Notably, **the Utah Code
contains no registered-owner liability presumption for parking**: a search for parking co-occurring
with "registered owner" or "owner of the vehicle" returns nothing. That machinery, where it exists,
is municipal.

## The pair, side by side

| | Duty | Kind | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stopped on reasonable suspicion | Disclose **name or date of birth** (§76-8-301.5) | Compelled **assertion** | Class B misdemeanour |
| — address, explanation of actions | Officer may demand (§77-7-15); **no offence for refusing** | — | none |
| Driving a vehicle | **Display licence** (§53-3-217) | **Document presentation** | Infraction |
| Paying a parking ticket | **None in state law** (§77-7-21(1)(b)) | — | — |

The traffic stop is the clearest case in this repo where the state compels identity information from
an ordinary person. Even there, the compulsion is to *say* a name and date of birth; the only
document duty rides on the privilege of driving, and it is satisfied by carrying a card rather than
by the state confirming anything. And the moment the encounter becomes a piece of paper on a
windscreen, the identity duty vanishes entirely.
